Methodology & sources

The map shows the 100 busiest U.S. domestic air city markets and the passenger journeys between every pair of them: 4,950 pairs in all. It uses the same data and definitions as the top 100 pairs table.

What is counted

  • Each BTS DB1C Market record is one passenger origin–destination journey within BTS market boundaries. A New York–Atlanta–Orlando connection counts only between New York and Orlando.
  • Both directions are combined, and a round trip normally counts once in each direction. These are journeys, not unique people.
  • DB1C is a 40% ticket sample; each sampled record is multiplied by 2.5. The 12 monthly files for reporting months July 2025–June 2026 are summed.
  • Trips that start and end in the same city market are excluded. Domestic includes Alaska, Hawaii, and U.S. territories; trips on international tickets are not counted.

Choosing the 100 markets

  • A city market's total is the sum of its journeys with every other market (all 1,000+ markets, not just the top 100). The 100 largest totals are mapped.
  • Pairs among these 100 markets carry 88% of all domestic journeys. A few pairs (183) have no sampled journeys and show as 0.
  • "% of journeys" in the list is the pair's share of the selected market's total, including its journeys to markets outside the top 100.
  • "With stops" is the share of the pair's journeys with a change of flight (two or more flight coupons).

Geography

  • Airports are grouped using BTS city markets, as in the table (e.g., New York City = JFK, EWR, LGA, HPN, ISP, SWF; Los Angeles = LAX, SNA, BUR, ONT, LGB). Orlando Sanford (SFB) and West Palm Beach (PBI) are separate BTS markets.
  • Each market is drawn as the Census Bureau's 2025 core-based statistical areas (CBSAs; metropolitan and micropolitan areas) that contain its airports, the same boundaries used in the housing cost map. Airports with under 0.25% of a market's journeys don't add an area.
  • Where two markets have airports in the same CBSA, the CBSA is split by county: a county with an airport goes to that airport's market, and the rest go to the larger market. This affects three CBSAs: Miami (Palm Beach County → West Palm Beach), Orlando (Seminole County → Sanford), and Riverside–San Bernardino (San Bernardino County → Los Angeles via ONT; Riverside County → Palm Springs).
  • St. Thomas is in no CBSA and is drawn as St. Thomas Island. A market's shape shows where its airports are, not where its travelers live.
  • Lines run between traffic-weighted airport locations along great circles. Line width shows journeys.
