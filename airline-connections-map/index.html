Methodology & sources

The map shows the 100 busiest U.S. domestic air city markets and the passenger journeys between every pair of them: 4,950 pairs in all. The matrix view shows the same 4,950 pairs as a grid. It uses the same data and definitions as the top 100 city-pairs table.

What is counted

  • Each BTS DB1C Market record is one passenger origin–destination journey within BTS market boundaries. A New York–Atlanta–Orlando connection counts only between New York and Orlando.
  • Both directions are combined, and a round trip normally counts once in each direction. These are journeys, not unique people.
  • DB1C is a 40% ticket sample; each sampled record is multiplied by 2.5. The 12 monthly files for reporting months July 2025–June 2026 are summed.
  • Trips that start and end in the same city market are excluded. Domestic includes Alaska, Hawaii, and U.S. territories; trips on international tickets are not counted.

Choosing the 100 markets

  • A city market's total is the sum of its journeys with every other market (all 1,000+ markets, not just the top 100). The 100 largest totals are mapped.
  • Pairs among these 100 markets carry 88% of all domestic journeys. A few pairs (183) have no sampled journeys and show as 0.
  • "% of total" is the pair's share of the selected market's total journeys, including its journeys to markets outside the top 100. "With stops" is the share of the pair's journeys with a change of flight (two or more flight coupons).

Reading the map

  • Circles sit at each market's airports (weighted by traffic). With no market selected, circle area shows the market's total journeys. With a market selected, circle area shows journeys with that market, scaled so its largest connection is the biggest circle, and color shows the share of the selected market's journeys.
  • Lines curve between markets; width shows journeys and tapers from the selected market toward the other end. With nothing selected, gray lines show the 25 busiest pairs.
  • The matrix groups markets by Census region of their main airport (Alaska and Hawaii, and Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, are shown separately), or orders them by rank.

Geography

  • Airports are grouped using BTS city markets, as in the table (e.g., New York City = JFK, EWR, LGA, HPN, ISP, SWF; Los Angeles = LAX, SNA, BUR, ONT, LGB). Orlando Sanford (SFB) and West Palm Beach (PBI) are separate BTS markets.
  • The shaded areas are the Census Bureau's 2025 core-based statistical areas (CBSAs; metropolitan and micropolitan areas) that contain each market's airports, the same boundaries used in the housing cost map. Airports with under 0.25% of a market's journeys don't add an area.
  • Where two markets have airports in the same CBSA, the CBSA is split by county: a county with an airport goes to that airport's market, and the rest go to the larger market. This affects three CBSAs: Miami (Palm Beach County → West Palm Beach), Orlando (Seminole County → Sanford), and Riverside–San Bernardino (San Bernardino County → Los Angeles via ONT; Riverside County → Palm Springs).
  • St. Thomas is in no CBSA and is drawn as St. Thomas Island. A market's area shows where its airports are, not where its travelers live.
