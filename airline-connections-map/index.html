    Methodology & sources

    Map shows the 100 busiest U.S. domestic air city markets and passenger journeys between 4,950 pairs in all. The matrix view shows the same 4,950 pairs as a grid, using the same data and definitions as the top 100 city-pairs table.

    • Each BTS DB1C Market record is one passenger origin–destination journey within BTS market boundaries. A New York–Atlanta–Orlando connection counts only between New York and Orlando, and so forth.
    • Both directions are combined. A round trip counts once in each direction.
    • DB1C is a 40% ticket sample; each sampled record is multiplied by 2.5. The 12 monthly files for reporting months July 2025–June 2026 are summed.
    • Trips that start and end in the same city market are excluded. Domestic includes Alaska, Hawaii, and U.S. territories; trips on international tickets are not included in the data set.
    • A city market’s total is the sum of its journeys with every other market (all 1,000+ markets, not just the top 100). The 100 largest totals are mapped.
    • Pairs among these 100 markets account for 88% of all domestic journeys. A few pairs (183) have no sampled journeys and show as 0.
    • “% of total” is the pair’s share of the selected market’s total journeys, including journeys to markets outside the top 100. “With stops” is the share of the pair’s journeys with a change of flight (two or more flight coupons).

    Circles sit at each market’s airports (weighted by traffic). If no market is selected, circle area is based on the market’s total journeys. If a market is selected, circle area is based on the number of journeys with that market, scaled such that its largest connection is the biggest circle. Color shows the share of the selected market’s journeys.

    Lines curve between markets; width shows journeys, tapering from the selected market toward the other end. When nothing is selected, gray lines show the 25 busiest pairs.

    The matrix groups markets by Census region of their main airport (Alaska and Hawaii, and Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, are shown separately), or orders them by rank.

    • Airports are grouped using BTS city markets, as in the table (e.g., New York City = JFK, EWR, LGA, HPN, ISP, SWF; Los Angeles = LAX, SNA, BUR, ONT, LGB). Orlando Sanford (SFB) and West Palm Beach (PBI) are separate BTS markets.
    • Shaded areas are the Census Bureau’s 2025 core-based statistical areas (CBSAs; metropolitan and micropolitan areas) that contain each market’s airports, excluding airports with under 0.25% of a market’s journeys.
    • Where two markets have airports in the same CBSA, the CBSA is split by county: a county with an airport goes to that airport’s market, and the rest go to the larger market. This affects three CBSAs: Miami (Palm Beach County → West Palm Beach), Orlando (Seminole County → Sanford), and Riverside–San Bernardino (San Bernardino County → Los Angeles via ONT; Riverside County → Palm Springs).
    • St. Thomas is in no CBSA and is drawn as St. Thomas Island.
